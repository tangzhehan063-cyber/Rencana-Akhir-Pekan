# Review the premise, hook and script

Review an original script before recording narration. For supplied or approved copy, use the same checks to identify proposed edits for the user's review. Keep the selected style's [production and acceptance checks](autopilot-production-qa.md).

1. **Choose a premise.** When the topic is open, compare three one-sentence premises. For each, identify the intended audience, what they would want to know, and one image or action that makes the idea concrete. Verify central factual claims against the source before choosing. Start from the assigned premise when the topic is fixed.
2. **Write the payoff.** State the expectation set by the title and opening, then identify the scene that fulfills it. In fiction, decide what changes for the character and what mystery can stay open. In an explainer, identify the demonstration or evidence that answers the opening question.
3. **Try three openings.** When drafting original copy, vary the approach. Start with a concrete event, a character's decision, or a consequence the audience can see. Read each aloud against its intended first image. Choose the version that lets the audience understand the situation and form a specific question quickly.
4. **Check each beat.** In the shot ledger, record what the audience sees, what they learn, and how this changes the next action or their expectations. Combine repetitive beats or give the repeated event a new consequence. Leave room to read clues, absorb reveals and anticipate outcomes.
5. **Try a cold read or rough playback.** When a fresh viewer is available, ask what question the opening raised, where their attention dropped or the story confused them, and what they understood from the ending. Otherwise, record an editorial read-through and mark it as such. Revise before recording and check the actual preview against the revised script.
6. **Learn from publication.** When audience analytics are available, map dips and spikes to the corresponding beats and note a revision to test. A spike can reflect interest or confusion. Use comparable audience segments and runtimes where available. [YouTube audience retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)

## Example from ABSEN

- **Opening:** Rudi is missing, yet the attendance machine thanks him every morning at seven. Show his name on the display and the empty space in front of it.
- **Escalation:** In a proposed revision, CCTV connects the repeated message to the lift stopping on floor four. Darto follows a lead. A schedule naming the security guard makes the danger personal.
- **Payoff:** The machine marks Darto as having clocked out while he is still on floor four. Give the audience time to read the notification and see his location.

## Research used for the review

- Predicting a trivia answer increased curiosity in an experiment. Use this as a reason to try an opening that gives viewers enough information to form a hypothesis. [Brod and Breitwieser, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6803639/)
- Rising suspense in film excerpts changed how viewers processed central and peripheral visual information. Stage the story-relevant cue where viewers can see it. [Bezdek et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2015.06.055)
- Narrative engagement includes understanding, attention, emotional engagement and presence. Ask about these separately when diagnosing a rough cut. These studies measured specific aspects of engagement. Use published-video analytics to evaluate actual retention. [Busselle and Bilandzic, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213260903287259)
