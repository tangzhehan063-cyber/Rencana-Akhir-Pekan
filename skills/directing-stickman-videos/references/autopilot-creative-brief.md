# Adapt the Code Engine brief to each video

Use this brief before drafting the script and choosing how to stage the video. Fill it from the user's request and available source material. Carry reasonable, reversible assumptions into the draft and identify them in the handoff. Update the brief when the script or preview exposes a better choice.

## Choose the approach

1. Identify what the audience should **understand, do, decide or feel** by the end. Write one primary outcome and specify what viewers already know. Use the outcome to choose a structure. A technology topic can become a tutorial, a comparison or a character story, depending on the outcome.
2. Match the opening to the ending. Identify one thing to show immediately, then describe the demonstration, evidence, result or dramatic moment that delivers on the opening. For a hybrid, choose which outcome drives the edit and give the secondary format only the time it needs to support that outcome.
3. Choose picture, pacing and sound around the actual beats. For each important moment, decide where the viewer should look, how long they need to absorb it and what sound matters there. Use the viewer's familiarity, delivery format, real narration and the selected B-style limits to decide timing. Choose a recurring sound or visual motif when it helps viewers recognize a pattern or notice a change.
4. Run the [script review](autopilot-script-review.md). Reuse its shot ledger for the production plan. Check at least one representative middle beat and the payoff in addition to the opening when reviewing the creative choices. For the production workflow, follow the [Code Engine sample and approval sequence](autopilot.md#sample--approval--full-film) and the [sound system](autopilot-sound-system.md).

## Examples by audience outcome

Use these as starting points. For an unfamiliar niche, derive the opening, progression and ending from the intended audience outcome using the same steps.

| Audience outcome | Opening → progression → ending | Picture, pace and sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand a concept | Show a familiar example → explain the mechanism in visible steps → apply it to a new example | Reveal diagram changes as they are explained. Hold long enough to read labels. Use sound to mark meaningful changes. |
| Complete a task | Show the finished result → demonstrate the steps and a likely mistake → check that the result works | Keep tool, hands or cursor visible. Give critical actions room and compress routine repetition. Retain useful action sounds. |
| Make a decision | Show the choice and why it matters → compare options against the same criteria → explain who each option suits | Use comparable framing or scales. Hold evidence long enough to inspect it. Keep the mix even across options. |
| Follow a factual story | Open with a documented consequence or question → trace causes and turning points → explain what the evidence supports | Use dated evidence, maps or timelines when relevant. Set the pace by what viewers need to connect. Distinguish reconstructions and unverified details in the presentation. |
| Enjoy a comedy | Make the premise clear → repeat or escalate the pattern with a variation → land the reversal or reaction | Give the setup and reaction their own space. Use framing, timing and selective silence to make the joke land. |
| Feel suspense | Show an anomaly → give the character a reason to act and add clues with consequences → resolve the immediate danger or show its outcome | Keep clues legible. Use framing and sound to direct attention. In ABSEN, the attendance chime can recur with a different name. In LANTAI 4, repeated framing can make a changed detail visible. |
| Consider a product or offer | Show a relevant use case → demonstrate a supported benefit and any material limitation → give a clear next action | Show the actual use and result. Give important conditions time to read and keep cues aligned with the demonstration. Verify claims before scripting. |

Honor the user's requested style and use its production contract for implementation. This brief describes creative decisions for the available production routes.
